# Photography shot list — Rian Cullet

The site currently ships licensed stock photography as an interim. It is
competent, but it is not Rian Cullet's material or Rian Cullet's floor.

For a company whose entire proposition is material quality and 31 years
of handling it, authentic photography is the single highest-value
upgrade available. A procurement manager can tell stock from a real
sorting floor, and the difference reads as credibility.

Everything below can be shot in a day on the premises with a decent
camera or a recent phone. No studio required.

---

## How to shoot it

**Light.** Daylight, always. Shoot near a shutter door or outside under
bright overcast. Glass lives on transmitted light: it must be lit
*through*, not flashed at. Direct on-camera flash kills it completely —
it flattens the colour and throws hard specular blowouts.

**Angle.** Get close and shoot slightly down into the material. Fill the
frame. A wide shot of a warehouse says "warehouse"; a tight shot of
cullet says "material".

**Depth.** Let the background fall off. A shallow plane of focus across a
pile makes the near fragments read as gemstones. That is the whole visual
argument of this site.

**Cleanliness.** Sweep the immediate area. Remove hoses, pallets, bottles,
tools, cups, paperwork. One stray plastic wrapper undoes the premium
read instantly.

**Do not** add filters, do not boost saturation, do not add a green cast
for "eco". The material supplies the colour. The site adds none.

**Format.** Largest resolution the camera offers, landscape and portrait
of each subject so crops are available. Send originals, not screenshots
or WhatsApp-compressed copies — WhatsApp will destroy the detail that
makes these work.

## The shots

| Slot | What to shoot | Frame |
|---|---|---|
| `hero-glass-cullet` | Mixed sorted cullet, green and amber and clear together. The most jewel-like pile on site. | 16:9 landscape, shot wide enough to crop |
| `intro-cullet-macro` | Very tight macro of a single handful. Edges, facets, transmitted light. | 4:5 portrait |
| `factory-cullet` | Cullet piles with the factory building visible behind them. Should read *plant* at a glance. | 4:5 portrait |
| `foreign-cullet` | Foreign (post-consumer) material as received, before sorting. Mixed, honest, not staged. | 4:5 portrait |
| `flint-cullet` | Sorted flint (white / clear) cullet, close enough to see it is colourless. | 4:5 portrait |
| `clear-glass` | Clear and white cullet only. Bright, high-key, lots of light through it. | 3:4 portrait |
| `amber-glass` | Amber cullet only. Warm, rich, backlit if possible. | 3:4 portrait |
| `green-glass` | Green cullet only. Deep and saturated, not yellow-green. | 3:4 portrait |
| `processing-sorting` | The work happening: sorting, grading, separating. Hands are welcome. | 3:2 landscape |

---

## No founder portrait

The founder section is typographic by design and needs no photograph.
Nothing on the shoot list covers it, and nothing in the layout is
waiting for it.

---

## Delivering

Name each file after the slot and drop it into:

```
wp-content/themes/rian-cullet/assets/img/
```

The theme finds it automatically and no template changes are needed. Send
originals; conversion to AVIF and WebP at the correct crops happens
during the build.
